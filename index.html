Free 3D reef processing for reef researchers

Send us a dive video of a reef transect. We turn it into a 3D model of the reef with benthic cover estimated by AI, free of charge. In return, you let us use your footage to improve the AI, and you label a few hundred points to teach it what your reef looks like.

The exchange

What you get, for each transect

  • A 3D model of the reef you can rotate in a web browser, coloured either by photo or by benthic group.
  • The 3D model as a file (.ply) that opens in CloudCompare or MeshLab, with the AI's class for every point.
  • Benthic cover as seen from above: live coral, dead coral, algae and rock, sand and rubble, other life. As a table and as a map.
  • A short video of the reconstruction, which you're welcome to use in talks and posts.

What we ask in return

  • Permission to use your footage, still frames from it, and your labels to train and test our AI model (details below).
  • About an hour of labelling: in a web page, you see a photo with a crosshair and choose what is under it. Each class has example photos. A few hundred points in total.
  • Honest feedback on whether the results match what you know about the reef.

This is what a 4-minute transect becomes: millions of 3D points in the reef's own colours, then coloured by the AI, green for live coral and orange for dead coral. You can turn and zoom it yourself in the browser.

Your labels matter most. The AI was trained mainly on Red Sea reefs. A few hundred points from your reef will teach it more than anything else we can do, and they also tell both of us how accurate it is on your reef.

How to film

The 3D model is built by matching details between overlapping video frames, so how you film decides how good it is.

  • Straight down, steady height. Keep the camera level and about 1–2 m above the reef.
  • Slow and continuous. Swim slowly along the transect without stopping, spinning or turning back. Each part of the reef should stay in view for a couple of seconds.
  • One transect per video, 2–10 minutes long. 1080p or 4K at 30 fps or more.
  • Turn off image stabilisation if your camera lets you (HyperSmooth, RockSteady and similar). It reshapes each frame and makes the 3D less accurate.
  • Steady light. Overcast days or deeper water work best. Bright sun in shallow water makes flickering light patterns that confuse the matching.
  • A scale object, if you can. A quadrat, scale bar or ruler of known size lying on the reef at a few points along the way. It lets us report areas in square metres. Without it, cover is still given as a percentage.
  • Normal colour is fine. If you filmed in a log or flat colour profile (for example DJI D-Log M or GoPro Flat), please tell us which one.

How to send your footage

  1. Put the original video files in a Google Drive folder. Please send the files straight off the camera, without editing or re-exporting.
  2. Share the folder with us. We'll send you the email address to share it with.
  3. Send us a short note, one per video:
Site name and GPS (or nearest place):

Processing takes about 5 hours per 5 minutes of video on our machine, and we check every result before sending it. Expect results within one to two weeks, depending on how many videos are waiting.

How we use your data

  • You keep ownership of your footage and of everything we make from it.
  • We use your footage, frames and labels to train and test the ReefSpark AI model. This is the only condition of the free processing.
  • We don't publish your footage, results or site locations without your permission. If you say yes to public use, we credit you as you ask.
  • Your labelling page shows a few dozen still frames from your video. It lives on this website at a link we send only to you, but the website is public, so anyone with that link could see those frames. We take it down when you've finished labelling.
  • You can ask us to delete your data at any time. We'll delete the footage, frames and labels, and won't use them in any future training. A model already trained with them can't be un-trained, but we won't train new versions with your data.
  • No fee, now or later, for the transects you send under this arrangement.

Please read before using the numbers: the cover figures are AI estimates and haven't yet been validated on reefs in your region. The AI sometimes mixes up live and dead coral. Use the results for exploration and pilot work, not as a replacement for a standard survey, until your labels tell us how accurate it is on your reef.
